You'll spend six dollars on a coffee without thinking. But a two dollar app? You'll read every review first. That's because nobody actually knows what anything is worth. So your brain takes a shortcut. It compares the price to whatever's next to it. Coffee sits next to other coffee. Six dollars feels normal. Apps sit next to free apps. Two dollars feels like a lot. Now imagine two sellers with the same ninety-nine dollar course. The first one puts it next to other courses, and the free videos on YouTube. Next to free, ninety-nine dollars looks expensive. The second one puts it next to the problem. Next to a two thousand dollar mistake, ninety-nine dollars looks cheap. Same course. Same price. Different comparison. Of course, the comparison has to be honest. If the problem isn't real, people can tell. But if you remember one thing, remember this. People don't judge your price. They judge what's next to it.
