The kid who sold candy will be much richer than those fuckass kids who snitched.
Most people think the rule followers win. Good grades, no trouble, a safe job.
But economists Ross Levine and Yona Rubinstein followed thousands of Americans from their teens into adulthood. The ones who went on to run their own incorporated businesses were more likely to have broken rules as teenagers. And they earned more than people with regular jobs.
This happens because the candy kid was learning things school never tested. He found out what people wanted. He set a price. He took the risk of getting caught. Every recess was a sales lesson.
The kids who snitched learned something too. How to get rewarded for following the rules.
Of course, the winners in that study were rule breakers who were also smart. Breaking rules on its own just gets you detention.
So if you were the candy kid, you were never the problem. You were early.
