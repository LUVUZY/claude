Giving people less time is one of the easiest ways to make more money.
Most sellers think a long deadline is generous. More time to think means more time to buy.
But researchers Suzanne Shu and Ayelet Gneezy tested it. People got a gift certificate. Some had two months to use it. Others had only three weeks.
With two months, about six percent used it. With three weeks, thirty-one percent did. Five times more, with less time.
This happens because a far deadline feels like someday, and someday never comes. A close deadline turns maybe into now.
It's the same reason that in a famous 1975 study, people rated cookies from a nearly empty jar as better than the exact same cookies from a full one.
Of course, a fake countdown destroys trust the moment buyers notice. The deadline has to be real.
So stop giving buyers forever. Forever is just a polite way of saying no.
