Adding a product nobody buys can almost triple the sales of the one you actually want to sell.
Most sellers think every option has to earn its spot. If nobody picks it, cut it.
But in a classic experiment, Dan Ariely tested an old Economist subscription offer. Web only, fifty-nine dollars. Print only, a hundred and twenty-five. Print and web, also a hundred and twenty-five.
Nobody chose print only. So he removed it.
With the useless option on the page, eighty-four percent chose print and web. Without it, only thirty-two percent did.
This happens because buyers don't know what a price should be. They judge by comparison. Next to print only, the bundle suddenly looks like a free gift.
It's called the decoy effect, and you'll see it everywhere, from popcorn sizes to phone plans.
Of course, your decoy still has to be a real option. Buyers can tell when a choice only exists to trick them.
Nobody buys your price. They buy the comparison you put next to it.
